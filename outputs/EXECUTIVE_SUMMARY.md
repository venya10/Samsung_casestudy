# Samsung MENA — Marketing Performance Review
**8 weeks · 8 subsidiaries · 8 channels · 6 devices · all figures AED**

**Position:** 247.3m AED of sales on 24.2m AED of media — a 10.2x marketing
efficiency ratio, with media at 9.8% of sales. The topline is healthy. The
allocation underneath it is not, and one number explains why.

---

## The headline: half the budget cannot be evaluated

**TV holds 48% of media spend (11.6m AED) and has no attributed sales or
conversions in this dataset.**

Not poor performance — *no measurement*. Any TV ROI quoted from this data would be
invented, so TV is excluded from every ranking in this analysis rather than scored
as a zero.

This is not a reason to cut TV. The panel model finds TV spend **positively and
significantly associated** with market sales (+5.0 AED per AED, 95% CI 0.4 to 9.6,
p=0.03) — exactly what you would expect from a broad-reach channel whose effect
last-click attribution cannot see.

**It is a reason to measure it.** A geo holdout or incrementality test on TV is the
single highest-value analytics investment available, and until it exists no
reallocation involving 48% of the budget is defensible in either direction.

---

## Where the money actually differs

| Dimension | Spread | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Between channels** | 2.1x → 23.4x ROAS | **11-fold** |
| Between markets | 9.7x → 10.8x MER | 11% |
| Between products | 19.0x → 20.6x ROAS | 8% |

Eight subsidiaries perform within 11% of each other; six devices within 8%. Channels
differ by more than an order of magnitude. **The entire optimisation opportunity is
channel mix.** Re-planning by market or by product would rearrange positions that
are already broadly equivalent.

Search takes under a fifth of budget and produces **43% of all sales**. Influencer
and B2B Roadshow together take 10% of budget and return under 3%.

**ROI on revenue flatters every channel.** The margin column applies a 22% blended
gross margin — the number a budget decision turns on. Two channels that look
positive on revenue fall below breakeven on margin. *Confirm this rate with
Finance: it scales that column proportionally.*

## Three findings

### 1. Influencer is below breakeven — as a channel, not as a roster

**Not one of the 24 creators returns above breakeven on gross margin.** The best
reaches 0.74x; the median is 0.48x. This cannot be fixed by dropping the worst few
names.

**Every creator sits in one follower tier** (891k–997k). There is no micro or
mid-tier presence at all — and that is where influencer efficiency usually lives,
because smaller accounts convert at a fraction of the fee.

**Action.** Do not renew at current rates. Commission a micro and mid-tier test
cohort before the next contracting cycle. Renegotiating the existing roster
optimises a channel that does not currently pay back.

### 2. Earned activity produces a third of sales at zero media cost

PR and Website carry no spend and generate **84.8m AED — 34.3% of all sales**.

**Earned is not free.** It is bought by brand investment made earlier — which is
precisely why the TV measurement gap matters commercially. If broad-reach spend is
what feeds this earned demand, cutting it to chase measurable channels would erode
the most efficient line in the business.

### 3. Only two channels are statistically distinguishable from zero

A panel model of weekly market sales on channel spend, with market fixed effects and
cluster-robust intervals (R²=0.74, n=64), finds only **Search** (p=0.006) and **TV**
(p=0.031) separable from no effect. Every other confidence interval crosses zero.

**That is the finding, not a shortcoming.** Eight weeks cannot rank channels on
differences this fine, and a table of point estimates without intervals would invite
exactly that error.

## Recommended actions

| When | Action | Owner | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Now** | Commission TV incrementality testing (geo holdout) | Marketing Director | Puts a number on 48% of budget |
| **Now** | Freeze influencer renewals at current rates | Influencer Manager | 1.56m AED under review |
| **This cycle** | Shift ~0.36m AED from Influencer and B2B Roadshow into Search and Retail | Media Lead | ~6.1m AED sales / ~1.3m margin |
| **This cycle** | Commission a micro/mid-tier influencer test cohort | Influencer Manager | Tests the only untested tier |
| **Next quarter** | Build channel-level attribution for TV and PR | Marketing Intelligence | Removes the largest blind spot |

**The reallocation is a direction, not an optimum.** Without a response curve there
is no way to estimate diminishing returns, so it assumes the receiving channel holds
its observed efficiency on incremental budget — the assumption most likely to be
wrong. Move a bounded share, measure, repeat.

**What not to do:** re-plan by market or product. Neither contains a material
misallocation.

## Assumptions and limits

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **8 weeks of data** | **No marketing mix model.** Carryover cannot be identified from 8 observations per market, so no channel *contribution* or incrementality is claimed anywhere — only measured efficiency and association |
| Gross margin | 22% blended, assumed. Scales every margin figure proportionally |
| TV and PR | No attributed revenue. Excluded from all ROI ranking rather than scored zero |
| Brand metrics | Awareness, intent, competitor SOV and sentiment appear per source row and vary by up to **39 points within a single market-week** — so they are not a market-level tracker. Averaged to market-week and reported as an indicative index. **Compare markets; do not read week-to-week movement** |
| Channels | Treated as mutually exclusive attribution buckets, so total sales is their sum |
| Market codes | SGE, SESAR, SEEG, SELV, SEMAG, SEPAK, SETK, SEIL — subsidiary codes. Expansions used in the dashboard are working labels, **unconfirmed** |
| Currency | AED throughout, no conversion applied |
| Social data | No platform breakdown and no organic metrics, so platform-level recommendations are out of scope |

**Competitive position is strong and stable:** Samsung leads share of voice in all
8 markets (≈32% against ≈10.5%). That lead sits in PR, which costs nothing — it is
the cheapest asset on this dashboard to defend.

The Early Warning System monitors seven risk classes weekly. Because 8 weeks give no
trailing baseline, it compares each subsidiary against its peer group in the same
week rather than against its own history — a signal available from week 1 rather
than one needing a quarter to warm up.
